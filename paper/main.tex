\begin{abstract}
% [1] Objetivo
This work presents an exploratory analysis of a publicly available
synthetic dataset of diabetes risk, with the goal of characterising
which of its predictors carry discriminative information before any
predictive model is considered.
% [2] Dados
The dataset comprises $N = 50{,}000$ records, $D = 22$ predictors and
$L = 3$ risk classes, with a strongly imbalanced class distribution in
which the \emph{Low} class holds only $0.94\%$ of the observations.
% [3] Metodos
We combine unconditional and class-conditional univariate statistics,
pairwise Pearson correlation and a principal component analysis (PCA)
implemented from first principles, without recourse to pre-made PCA
routines.
% [4] Resultado principal
Two glycemic markers, fasting blood sugar and HbA1c, separate the risk
classes almost by themselves, yet the first two principal components
account for only $15.2\%$ of the total variance. Three independent
lines of evidence, an excess kurtosis of $-1.2$ in 19 of the 20
quantitative predictors, a median absolute pairwise correlation of
$0.003$, and an eigenvalue spectrum flat at unity, converge on the same
conclusion: the predictors were drawn independently from bounded
uniform distributions, and the only structural relationship the data
contain is the algebraic identity that defines the body mass index.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
exploratory data analysis, principal component analysis, synthetic
data, diabetes risk, class separability
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% --- P1: diabetes como problema de saude publica + deteccao precoce.
% Referencias externas entram aqui (IDF/ADA).
Diabetes mellitus is one of the largest public health burdens
worldwide \cite{idf2021}. In 2024, 589 million adults aged 20 to 79
years, one in every nine, were living with the condition, and this
figure is projected to reach 853 million by 2050, a 45\% rise against
a projected population growth of 25\%. A further 252 million adults
are estimated to be unaware that they have it. The disease accounted
for 3.4 million deaths and for at least USD 1 trillion in health
expenditure in that single year, which is what makes early detection
consequential: it opens a window for treatment and lifestyle
intervention before microvascular and cardiovascular complications
become established.

% --- P2: por que analise exploratoria antes de modelagem.
% Este paragrafo justifica a existencia do trabalho.
Fitting a classifier without a prior exploratory analysis makes it
impossible to tell whether the performance obtained reflects genuine
clinical patterns or artefacts of the data. Descriptive statistics and
linear projections expose, beforehand, redundancy among predictors,
disparities of scale, outliers and the degree of overlap between the
classes. Without that map of the underlying structure, neither the
success nor the failure of a model can be diagnosed.

% --- P3: datasets sinteticos em saude. ESTE e o paragrafo que
% diferencia o trabalho: privacidade, volume, ensino, e as limitacoes.
% Prepara a discussao dos resultados e a conclusao.
The use of synthetic datasets in health care has grown as a way of
circumventing strict patient privacy constraints, of compensating for
the scarcity of records and of enabling educational applications
\cite{synthetic2021}. That convenience, however, carries the risk that
the generating process fails to reproduce the complex physiological
interdependencies present in real clinical populations. When
multivariate relationships are not preserved, algorithms trained on
such data tend to learn mathematical artefacts rather than legitimate
biological mechanisms. It is in this context that the dataset analysed
here belongs, its origin being explicitly synthetic \cite{kaggle2024}.

% --- P4: objetivo e organizacao do artigo.
This work presents an exploratory and multivariate characterisation of
a synthetic dataset of diabetes risk, assessing its statistical
consistency and its clinical fidelity before any modelling step. We
investigate the dependencies among predictors and determine to what
extent the risk classes are structurally separable, both in the
original predictor space and under projections of maximum variance.
The remainder of this paper is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the dataset and the analytical
methods. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports and discusses the results.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{subsec:dataset}

% Fonte, natureza sintetica, N, D, L.
The dataset was obtained from Kaggle \cite{kaggle2024} and is
explicitly described by its authors as a large-scale \emph{synthetic}
healthcare dataset. The raw file holds 41 columns, from which we
discard \texttt{Patient\_ID}, a row identifier carrying no
physiological information, and \texttt{Diabetes\_Risk\_Score}, the
continuous score from which the categorical label is derived. This
leaves $N = 50{,}000$ observations and $D = 22$ predictors, of which 20
are quantitative and two (\texttt{Country} and \texttt{Gender}) are
categorical. The class label \texttt{Diabetes\_Risk} takes $L = 3$
values.

% Distribuicao de classes: comentar o desbalanceamento (Low < 1%).
Table~\ref{tab:classdist} reports the class distribution.
The disparity between the 36{,}593 observations of class \emph{High}
and the 470 of class \emph{Low}, a ratio of 78 to 1 and a mere
$0.94\%$ of the sample, imposes structural limits on the analysis. It
renders the class-conditional estimates of the minority class
unstable, and it would compromise the evaluation of any future
classifier, since a naive model that constantly predicts \emph{High}
would already reach $73\%$ accuracy.

\subsection{Pre-processing}
\label{subsec:preproc}

% Padronizacao e dropna, com a limitacao dos 33%.
Standardising the variables to zero mean and unit variance before the
principal component analysis is mandatory, since the raw predictors
operate on widely disparate scales (mg/dL, kg, cm, years); without
that correction the variables of larger magnitude would dominate the
covariance matrix and the analysis would describe the units of
measurement rather than the intrinsic structure of the data.
Furthermore, because the covariance matrix requires complete records,
the rows holding missing values were removed. This exclusion reduced
the set from $50{,}000$ to $33{,}689$ observations, that is, $32.6\%$
of the data discarded, and it establishes a methodological limitation:
the multivariate analysis describes a complete subset and not the
entire cohort. The univariate and bivariate statistics, in contrast,
use every observation available for each predictor.

\subsection{Analytical methods}
\label{subsec:methods}

% Embasamento teorico exigido pelo enunciado. Definir cada estatistica
% e dizer o que ela informa.
For each predictor $d$ we compute the mean $\mu_d$, the standard
deviation $\sigma_d$ and the skewness $\gamma_d$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_d = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\left(\frac{x_{n,d}-\mu_d}{\sigma_d}\right)^{3},
\label{eq:skew}
\end{equation}
which quantifies the imbalance between the tails of a distribution and
vanishes for any density symmetric about its mean,
together with the excess kurtosis, the analogous fourth-order moment
minus three, and their class-conditional counterparts $\mu_{d|l}$,
$\sigma_{d|l}$ and $\gamma_{d|l}$ over the $N_l$ observations of each
class $l$. Skewness vanishes for any symmetric distribution and so
cannot by itself distinguish a Gaussian from a uniform density, whereas
the excess kurtosis separates them cleanly: it is $0$ for the Gaussian
and $-6/5$ for the uniform. We therefore report both.

% Correlacao de Pearson: dizer o que capta e o que NAO capta.
Linear dependence between pairs of predictors is quantified by the
Pearson correlation coefficient
\begin{equation}
\rho_{d_i,d_j} =
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{d_i}-\mu_{d_i})(X_{d_j}-\mu_{d_j})\right]}
{\sigma_{d_i}\sigma_{d_j}},
\label{eq:pearson}
\end{equation}
which captures linear association only. A vanishing $\rho$ does not
imply independence in general, so we treat the correlation matrix as
one piece of evidence among three rather than as a conclusion.

% Poder discriminativo: definir a metrica usada, pois nao e padrao.
To rank predictors by their ability to separate classes on their own,
we use the ratio between the range of the class-conditional means and
the unconditional standard deviation,
\begin{equation}
\delta_d = \frac{\max_l \mu_{d|l} - \min_l \mu_{d|l}}{\sigma_d},
\label{eq:discrim}
\end{equation}
so that $\delta_d$ expresses the class separation in units of the
predictor's own spread and is comparable across predictors measured in
different physical units.

% PCA implementado do zero: o enunciado EXIGE que se diga isso.
Finally, the principal component analysis was implemented from
scratch, without pre-made PCA functions or libraries, following the
standard chain \cite{jolliffe2002}: standardisation, estimation of the
covariance matrix $\mathbf{S}$, eigendecomposition
$\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{V}^{\top}$, and
projection of the observations onto the two eigenvectors of largest
eigenvalue. The only library routine used is the symmetric
eigensolver.

% O achado: quase tudo e uniforme, nao gaussiano.
Nearly every predictor shows a practically null skewness, ranging from
$-0.015$ to $+0.008$, with the notable exception of BMI
($\gamma = 0.432$). Such symmetry, however, does not imply normality,
since a uniform density is strictly symmetric as well; the decisive
quantity is the kurtosis. Nineteen of the twenty predictors have an
excess kurtosis between $-1.21$ and $-1.19$, matching the theoretical
value of $-6/5$ expected for a uniform distribution. This is
corroborated by the histograms of Fig.~\ref{fig:hist}, which are
visibly flat and truncated at round limits chosen for convenience
rather than at physiological ones: age from 18 to 90 years, heart rate
from 50 to 120 bpm, triglycerides from 60 to 400~mg/dL and sleep from
3 to 10 hours. The reading these figures impose is that the predictors
do not reflect measurements taken from real patients, but were drawn
from predefined uniform distributions. This is the first of three
analytical signals that will converge in Section~\ref{subsec:pca}.

Body mass index escapes this otherwise general artificial pattern,
showing a positive skewness and a kurtosis of $-0.45$ against the
$-1.2$ of the remaining variables. The divergence arises because BMI
was not drawn by the generator but computed algebraically as the
quotient $\mathrm{weight}/\mathrm{height}^2$, and the ratio of two
independent uniform variables is not itself uniform. The exception
therefore confirms that reading rather than weakening it: BMI is the
one predictor the generator did not draw.

% A resposta direta a pergunta do enunciado.
The answer to whether any predictor separates the classes on its own
is affirmative, and two markers stand out: fasting blood sugar
($\delta = 1.44$) and HbA1c ($\delta = 1.38$). The discriminative
capacity then falls substantially, to age ($\delta = 0.84$), BMI
($\delta = 0.61$) and weight ($\delta = 0.57$). The remaining fifteen
predictors all stay below $\delta = 0.5$, and ten of them below
$\delta = 0.1$, a range in which the separation between groups is
indistinguishable from noise.

% Os numeros clinicos que valem citar.
From a clinical standpoint, the predictors with the greatest
separating power display values and progressions that make full
physiological sense \cite{ada2024}. HbA1c rises from $5.67\%$ in the
low-risk class to $8.86\%$ in the high-risk one, which is exactly the
transition from the normal to the diabetic range, while fasting blood
sugar goes from $88.4$ to $152.8$~mg/dL, comfortably crossing the
diagnostic threshold of 126~mg/dL. Age advances from $38.8$ to $56.5$
years and BMI evolves from $25.6$ to $31.9$, a clear progression from
overweight to obesity. Under this strict reading of isolated means,
the dominant predictors are the clinically correct ones.

% As tres criticas. Isto e leitura critica, nao descricao: vale ponto.
Three observations qualify this apparent clinical fidelity.
First, casual blood glucose has a practically null discriminative
power ($\delta = 0.020$) and behaves as mere noise, whereas fasting
blood sugar dominates the separation ($\delta = 1.44$). Since both
measure the same physiological substrate and move together in a real
patient, the generator evidently derived the labels from one and
ignored the other.

Second, waist circumference, although an established marker of
metabolic and cardiovascular risk, does not separate the classes in
this synthetic set ($\delta = 0.041$). In further contradiction with
physiology, its mean value decreases slightly as the diabetic risk
increases, from $100.85$ to $99.91$~cm.

Third, mean height decreases from $173.7$ to $169.4$~cm as risk rises,
with $\delta = 0.30$, which has no medical basis whatsoever. This
distortion is a strictly algebraic consequence of BMI driving the
assignment of the labels: at equal weight, shorter individuals have a
mathematically larger BMI, so height inherits an inverted correlation
with the disease that it would never hold in a real population.

The correlation matrix approaches an identity matrix closely, with a
median absolute off-diagonal correlation of $0.003$. Only two pairs
depart from this pattern of total independence: BMI with weight
($\rho = +0.843$) and BMI with height ($\rho = -0.516$). These two
coefficients, however, reflect nothing but the mathematical definition
of the index itself, and are not an empirical or biological finding.
Consistently with uniform and independent predictors, the point clouds
of Fig.~\ref{fig:scatter} form rectangles devoid of latent structure,
and no pair of variables separates the classes visually. This absence
of linear dependence is the second signal.

% O achado mais interessante da secao.
The least plausible finding of the correlation matrix is that the only
two predictors with relevant discriminative power are statistically
uncorrelated with each other ($\rho = -0.005$). In a real patient
HbA1c and fasting blood sugar are strongly correlated, since the
former integrates the average glycaemia of the preceding weeks. Here
the disease label depends on both variables along mathematically
independent paths, which is physiologically impossible and is
explained only by the rules of the generating process, and it is the
sharpest illustration of that second signal.

% A assinatura dos autovalores: terceiro sinal, vindo de um terceiro

After standardisation the eigenvalues sum to 20, so a set of mutually
uncorrelated predictors would yield twenty eigenvalues equal to $1$.
That is very nearly what is observed: components PC2 to PC19 lie
between $0.956$ and $1.037$, confined to a band of less than $5\%$
around unity. Such a flat spectrum is the classical signature of an
absence of linear structure. It constitutes the third independent
signal, produced by a third statistical method, pointing at the same
fact that the kurtosis analysis and the correlation matrix had already
indicated.

% As duas excecoes sao a mesma coisa: a identidade do IMC.
The only departures from the flatness of the spectrum are the first
component ($\lambda = 1.994$) and the last one ($\lambda = 0.0123$).
The first concentrates its loadings almost exclusively on BMI
($-0.706$), weight ($-0.602$) and height ($+0.372$), everything else
staying below $0.02$ in absolute value, while the last mirrors exactly
the same triple with BMI $+0.708$, weight $-0.603$ and height
$+0.368$. The reading is that the analysis isolated, unprompted, the
algebraic identity by which BMI is strictly derived from weight and
height. The largest variance of the set therefore lies in the plane
spanned by these three variables, and the near-null eigenvalue
measures the almost deterministic dependence among them, almost and
not exactly because the relation is non-linear and the method captures
only its best linear approximation. It is the single structural
relation present in the data.

% Responder LITERALMENTE as tres perguntas do enunciado.
Answering the three questions directly, the classes are not well
separated in the projection, and are in fact separated \emph{worse}
than in the univariate analysis of HbA1c or fasting blood sugar.
Second, no linear decision boundary is visible that would distinguish
the risk groups in the projected space. Finally, \emph{Moderate}
overlaps \emph{High} heavily, while \emph{Low} becomes practically
invisible, both because of its small sample size, only 470 points, and
because it occupies the very same region as the other two.

% Paragrafo de fechamento: a tensao entre os dois achados.
The apparent contradiction between the strong signal found in the
univariate analysis and the absence of separation under PCA dissolves
once it is understood that the two measure distinct properties. The
first two components explain only $15.2\%$ of the variance because the
spectrum is flat and no dominant direction exists to concentrate it.
The projection fails to separate the classes for a different reason:
the method maximises variance, not separability, and when the
predictors are almost all independent there is no high-variance
direction in which the class information happens to lie. Moreover, the
single high-variance direction the set does possess, that of BMI, is
merely an algebraic relation with no discriminative content of its
own. PCA did not fail; it correctly reported that there is no
multivariate structure to report.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The exploratory analysis produced three independent pieces of evidence
that converge on the same fact: the uniform kurtosis of nineteen of
the twenty predictors, the median of $0.003$ among the off-diagonal
correlations, and the flat eigenvalue spectrum. Together they indicate
that the predictors were sampled from independent uniform
distributions over round numerical intervals, and that the diagnostic
label was assigned afterwards as a function of a restricted group of
variables, chiefly fasting blood sugar, HbA1c, age and BMI.
Consequently the only structural interdependence present in this
dataset is strictly algebraic, with no grounding in real physiology.

In practical terms, a synthetic dataset with these characteristics
serves perfectly well to exercise the methodological flow of data
analysis, which is the scope and the objective of this work. It is,
on the other hand, strictly inadequate as a basis for clinical
conclusions or for realistically testing multivariate methods,
precisely because the covariance those algorithms exist to exploit was
suppressed at generation time. A classifier trained here would reach a
very high apparent performance while relying on two predictors alone,
and that behaviour would not transfer to real medical records.
Recording and documenting this structural artificiality is, in itself,
the most robust result this exploratory analysis delivers.
